Apolipoprotein B (ApoB)

ApoB is the structural apolipoprotein carried by atherogenic lipoprotein particles. Because each major atherogenic particle carries one ApoB molecule, the blood concentration of ApoB provides an estimate of particle number rather than the cholesterol mass carried inside those particles.

Interpretation boundary

Particle number is not the whole risk profile

ApoB can improve cardiovascular risk assessment in selected patients, but risk still depends on age, blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, kidney function, family history and other factors.

Educational content only. Laboratory interpretation should be integrated with clinical context.

01

What it measures

ApoB estimates the number of circulating atherogenic particles, including LDL, VLDL remnants, IDL and Lp(a)-containing particles.

02

Why it differs from LDL-C

LDL-C reflects cholesterol content, whereas ApoB reflects particle count. Discordance can occur when particles carry different amounts of cholesterol.

03

Guideline context

The 2026 AHA/ACC dyslipidemia guideline includes selective ApoB measurement to improve risk assessment and guide management in appropriate individuals.

04

Key limitation

ApoB is a risk marker and treatment target in some settings; it does not identify plaque location or directly measure an individual clinical event probability by itself.
