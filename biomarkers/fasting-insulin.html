Fasting insulin

Fasting insulin measures circulating insulin after a fasting interval and can provide context about pancreatic insulin secretion and metabolic physiology. It is not, by itself, a standard diagnostic test for prediabetes or diabetes.

InsulinMetabolismPancreatic beta cellsContext-dependent

Interpretation boundary

No universal cutoff for “insulin resistance”

Assays and reference intervals vary, and fasting insulin should be interpreted with fasting glucose, HbA1c, clinical context and—when appropriate—other metabolic assessments.

Educational content only. Laboratory interpretation should be integrated with qualified clinical care.

01

What it measures

Circulating insulin concentration during a fasting state, reflecting a balance between pancreatic secretion, hepatic clearance and metabolic demand.

02

Insulin resistance context

Higher insulin levels can occur when more insulin is needed to maintain glucose homeostasis, but fasting insulin alone does not diagnose insulin resistance.

03

Diagnostic distinction

NIDDK identifies blood glucose tests such as HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose for diagnosing prediabetes; fasting insulin is not a standalone diagnostic criterion.

04

Key limitation

Insulin secretion is dynamic and influenced by recent diet, medications, sleep, stress, body composition and metabolic disease.
