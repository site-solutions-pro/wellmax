Lipoprotein(a) — Lp(a)

Lp(a) is an apoB-containing lipoprotein whose concentration is largely genetically determined. Elevated values are associated with higher risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and calcific aortic valve disease, while interpretation depends on assay method, units and the broader risk profile.

Guideline context

Often measured once to reveal inherited risk

The 2026 AHA/ACC dyslipidemia guideline recommends Lp(a) measurement at least once to identify individuals at higher ASCVD risk. Elevated Lp(a) is a risk-enhancing factor, not a standalone diagnosis of future cardiovascular events.

Educational content only. Laboratory interpretation should be integrated with clinical context.

01

What it measures

A cholesterol-carrying particle that contains ApoB-100 plus apolipoprotein(a), with circulating levels strongly influenced by inherited LPA genetics.

02

Risk association

Higher concentrations are linked to greater risk of coronary disease, stroke and aortic stenosis through atherogenic, inflammatory and thrombotic pathways.

03

Units matter

Lp(a) may be reported in nmol/L or mg/dL. These are not universally interchangeable because particle mass varies with apo(a) isoform size.

04

Context matters

Overall cardiovascular risk still depends on LDL/ApoB burden, blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, kidney function, age, family history and other factors.
