Insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1)

IGF-1 is produced mainly by the liver in response to growth hormone signaling and is used as a relatively stable marker of the GH axis. Interpretation depends strongly on age, sex, nutritional status, liver function and the clinical question.

GH axisEndocrinologyAge-dependentLiver biology

Interpretation boundary

Not a standalone diagnosis of GH status

IGF-1 can support evaluation of GH excess or deficiency, but normal or abnormal values do not replace endocrine history, examination and—in some settings—dynamic stimulation testing.

Educational content only. Laboratory interpretation should be integrated with qualified endocrine care.

01

What it reflects

Integrated GH-axis signaling over time, with less short-term pulsatility than circulating growth hormone itself.

02

Age dependence

Reference intervals change substantially across childhood, puberty and adulthood, so age-adjusted interpretation is essential.

03

What can alter it

Nutrition, liver disease, kidney disease, thyroid status, sex hormones and systemic illness can influence IGF-1 independently of primary pituitary disease.

04

Key limitation

An IGF-1 value cannot by itself establish performance, recovery, longevity or body-composition benefit.
