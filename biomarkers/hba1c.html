HbA1c

Hemoglobin A1c reflects glycation of hemoglobin and is used to estimate longer-term glucose exposure. It is widely used for diabetes screening, diagnosis and monitoring, but interpretation depends on red-cell lifespan, assay context and clinical circumstances.

Interpretation boundary

A summary signal, not a continuous glucose trace

HbA1c compresses weeks of glucose exposure into one laboratory value. It does not show glycemic variability, timing of excursions or the cause of abnormal glucose regulation.

Educational content only. Laboratory interpretation should be integrated with clinical context.

01

What it measures

The percentage of hemoglobin with glucose attached, reflecting average glycemic exposure over the lifespan of circulating red blood cells.

02

Clinical use

CDC and major diabetes guidelines recognize A1c as one of the tests used for screening and diagnosis of prediabetes and diabetes, and for ongoing monitoring.

03

Key limitations

Conditions affecting red-cell turnover, hemoglobin variants, blood loss, transfusion and some kidney or liver disorders can alter interpretation.

04

Context matters

Discordance between HbA1c, glucose measurements and symptoms should prompt evaluation of assay and biological context rather than reliance on one number.
