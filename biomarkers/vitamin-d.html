25-hydroxyvitamin D — 25(OH)D

Serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D is the main laboratory indicator used to assess vitamin D status. It reflects vitamin D from sunlight, foods and supplements, but interpretation is influenced by assay variability, physiology and the clinical question being asked.

Interpretation boundary

No single universal “optimal” target for every person

NIH notes that thresholds for deficiency and adequacy depend on evidence framework, and the Endocrine Society does not recommend routine testing in healthy people without a specific indication.

Educational content only. Laboratory interpretation should be integrated with clinical context.

01

What it measures

25(OH)D is the primary circulating form used to estimate vitamin D status because it reflects both endogenous production and intake.

02

Why it matters

Vitamin D supports calcium and phosphate homeostasis, bone mineralization and normal skeletal physiology.

03

Assay limitation

Laboratory methods vary, so values should be interpreted with the assay, units and clinical setting in mind.

04

Key boundary

A higher 25(OH)D concentration is not automatically better; excessive vitamin D exposure can be harmful.
