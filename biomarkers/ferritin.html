Ferritin

Ferritin is an intracellular iron-storage protein measured in blood as part of iron assessment. Low values can support iron deficiency, while high values are not specific for iron overload because ferritin can also rise with inflammation, liver disease and other conditions.

Interpretation boundary

Storage marker and acute-phase reactant

Ferritin should be interpreted alongside hemoglobin, transferrin saturation, inflammatory context, symptoms and clinical history rather than as a standalone diagnosis.

Educational content only. Laboratory interpretation should be integrated with qualified clinical care.

01

What it measures

Circulating ferritin correlates with body iron stores and is commonly included in iron-status evaluation.

02

Low ferritin

Low values can support depleted iron stores and iron deficiency, especially when integrated with other blood indices.

03

High ferritin

Elevated ferritin can occur with iron overload, but also with inflammation, liver disease, obesity, alcohol-related disease, malignancy and other conditions.

04

Key limitation

Laboratory reference intervals vary. A single value does not identify the cause of abnormal iron physiology.
