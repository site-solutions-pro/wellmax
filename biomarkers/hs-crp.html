High-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP)

hs-CRP measures low concentrations of C-reactive protein, an acute-phase protein produced largely by the liver in response to inflammatory signaling. It can help refine cardiovascular risk in selected contexts, but it is nonspecific and can rise with infection, injury and many inflammatory states.

Interpretation boundary

Inflammation signal, not a diagnosis

hs-CRP does not identify the source of inflammation. Acute illness, injury and transient inflammatory conditions can raise the value and reduce its usefulness for chronic cardiovascular risk interpretation.

Educational content only. Laboratory interpretation should be integrated with clinical context.

01

What it measures

A highly sensitive assay for circulating C-reactive protein, allowing detection of lower concentrations than standard CRP testing.

02

Cardiovascular context

Prospective studies and prevention trials established hs-CRP as a risk-associated inflammatory biomarker used in selected cardiovascular risk frameworks.

03

Major limitation

CRP is nonspecific. Infection, trauma, autoimmune disease and other inflammatory conditions can influence results.

04

Best interpreted with

Lipid burden, blood pressure, glycemic status, smoking exposure, kidney function, family history and overall clinical context.
