Riboflavin (vitamin B2)

Riboflavin (vitamin B2) is found in milk, eggs, meat, fish, mushrooms and fortified grains. It gives supplements and urine a bright yellow color.

Class
Water-soluble vitamin (B2)
Evidence summary
Deficiency correction: established. Migraine prevention: guideline-supported (level B) based on small trials.
Review status
Agent draft, 2026-10-31. Scientific review required.

What it does in the body

Riboflavin is converted to FAD and FMN, coenzymes used by mitochondria to produce energy and by enzymes that activate vitamin B6 and folate.

Who may be at risk of deficiency

  • Vegans and people who consume little dairy.
  • Athletes who are vegetarian, in some studies.
  • Pregnant and lactating women in low-income settings.
  • People with alcohol use disorder.
  • Deficiency causes sore throat, cracked lips and corners of the mouth, and skin changes.

Forms in supplements

Supplements contain riboflavin or riboflavin 5'-phosphate.

What human trials show

  • RandomizedA placebo-controlled trial in 55 people with migraine found that high-amount riboflavin reduced attack frequency over three months.
  • GuidelineThe American Academy of Neurology and American Headache Society (2012) rated riboflavin as 'probably effective' (level B) for migraine prevention.
  • RandomizedTrials in other conditions are few and small.

Observational evidence

  • Riboflavin status interacts with a common MTHFR gene variant; low riboflavin may raise blood pressure in people with that variant, an area still under study.

Safety, upper limits and interactions

  • No tolerable upper limit has been set; absorption is limited at high intakes.
  • Bright yellow urine is harmless.

Reference values (NIH, adults)

ReferenceValue
Men (RDA)1.3 mg/day
Women (RDA)1.1 mg/day
Tolerable upper limitNot established (N/A)

Regulatory context

  • United States: dietary supplements do not need FDA approval for safety or effectiveness before sale; manufacturers are responsible for safety and label claims.
  • Brazil: food supplements are regulated by Anvisa (RDC 243/2018 and its normative instructions), which define permitted ingredients, minimum and maximum amounts and allowed claims.

Limits of the evidence

Migraine evidence rests on a few small trials. Riboflavin's effect size compared with prescription preventives is not well defined.

This profile is educational. It does not recommend doses, products or brands. Talk to a health professional before starting supplements, especially if you take medicines, are pregnant or have a chronic condition.
