Biotin (vitamin B7)

Biotin (vitamin B7) is found in eggs, organ meats, fish, seeds, nuts and some vegetables. Gut bacteria also produce some biotin.

Class
Water-soluble vitamin (B7)
Evidence summary
Deficiency correction: established. Hair and nails in people without deficiency: evidence unavailable. Lab-test interference: documented FDA warning.
Review status
Agent draft, 2026-10-31. Scientific review required.

What it does in the body

Biotin is a cofactor for five carboxylase enzymes involved in fatty-acid synthesis, glucose production and amino-acid breakdown. It also plays a role in gene regulation.

Who may be at risk of deficiency

  • Deficiency is rare.
  • Biotinidase deficiency, an inherited condition detected by newborn screening.
  • Regular consumption of raw egg whites, which contain avidin, a protein that binds biotin.
  • Long-term use of some anticonvulsants and alcohol use disorder.
  • Signs include thinning hair, a red scaly rash around the eyes, nose and mouth, and brittle nails.

Forms in supplements

Supplements contain d-biotin, often in amounts far above the adequate intake, especially in hair, skin and nail products.

What human trials show

  • Case reportsA review of published cases found that biotin improved hair or nails only in people with an underlying deficiency or specific conditions.
  • Evidence unavailableNo randomized trial has shown that biotin improves hair growth in people without deficiency.
  • RandomizedHigh-dose biotin was studied in progressive multiple sclerosis; a large phase III trial (SPI2) did not confirm benefit.

Observational evidence

  • EVIDENCE_UNAVAILABLE: no consistent observational data link usual biotin intake with disease risk.

Safety, upper limits and interactions

  • The FDA has warned that biotin can cause falsely high or low results in some lab tests, including troponin (used to diagnose heart attacks) and thyroid tests.
  • People should tell their clinician and laboratory if they take biotin before blood tests.
  • No tolerable upper limit has been set.

Reference values (NIH, adults)

ReferenceValue
Adults (Adequate Intake)30 mcg/day
Tolerable upper limitNot established (N/A)

Regulatory context

  • United States: dietary supplements do not need FDA approval for safety or effectiveness before sale; manufacturers are responsible for safety and label claims.
  • Brazil: food supplements are regulated by Anvisa (RDC 243/2018 and its normative instructions), which define permitted ingredients, minimum and maximum amounts and allowed claims.

Limits of the evidence

Popular claims about hair and nails rest on case reports in deficient people. Lab interference is a real safety issue at the high amounts common in beauty products.

This profile is educational. It does not recommend doses, products or brands. Talk to a health professional before starting supplements, especially if you take medicines, are pregnant or have a chronic condition.
