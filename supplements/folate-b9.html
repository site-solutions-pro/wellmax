Folate and folic acid (vitamin B9)

Folate is the natural form of vitamin B9 in leafy greens, legumes and liver. Folic acid is the synthetic form used in supplements and in fortified flour in many countries, including Brazil and the United States.

Class
Water-soluble vitamin (B9)
Evidence summary
Neural tube defect prevention: established by randomized trials. Other outcomes in replete adults: not shown.
Review status
Agent draft, 2026-10-31. Scientific review required.

What it does in the body

Folate is needed to make DNA and RNA and for cell division, which makes it essential during pregnancy and for red blood cell formation. It works with B12 to convert homocysteine to methionine.

Who may be at risk of deficiency

  • People planning pregnancy or in early pregnancy, when needs rise.
  • People with alcohol use disorder.
  • People with malabsorption, such as celiac disease.
  • Some medicines, including methotrexate and certain anticonvulsants, interfere with folate.
  • Deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia.

Forms in supplements

Supplements contain folic acid or 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF). Folic acid has the strongest trial evidence for neural tube defect prevention.

What human trials show

  • RandomizedThe MRC Vitamin Study showed that folic acid before conception and in early pregnancy prevented about 72% of recurrent neural tube defects.
  • GuidelineThe US Preventive Services Task Force (2023) recommends daily folic acid for everyone who could become pregnant.
  • RandomizedFolic acid trials that lowered homocysteine did not reduce heart attacks in people without deficiency.

Observational evidence

  • Mandatory flour fortification was followed by declines in neural tube defects in several countries, including Brazil.

Safety, upper limits and interactions

  • The upper limit applies only to synthetic folic acid, not to food folate.
  • High folic acid can mask the anemia of B12 deficiency while nerve damage progresses.
  • Folate interacts with methotrexate, some anticonvulsants and sulfasalazine.

Reference values (NIH, adults)

ReferenceValue
Adults (RDA)400 mcg DFE/day
Pregnancy (RDA)600 mcg DFE/day
Tolerable upper limit (folic acid only)1,000 mcg/day

Regulatory context

  • United States: dietary supplements do not need FDA approval for safety or effectiveness before sale; manufacturers are responsible for safety and label claims.
  • Brazil: food supplements are regulated by Anvisa (RDC 243/2018 and its normative instructions), which define permitted ingredients, minimum and maximum amounts and allowed claims.

Limits of the evidence

DFE (dietary folate equivalents) account for the higher absorption of folic acid than food folate. The value of 5-MTHF versus folic acid for neural tube prevention is less studied.

This profile is educational. It does not recommend doses, products or brands. Talk to a health professional before starting supplements, especially if you take medicines, are pregnant or have a chronic condition.
