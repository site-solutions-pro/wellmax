Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine)

Vitamin B6 is a group of related compounds (pyridoxine, pyridoxal, pyridoxamine) found in fish, poultry, potatoes, chickpeas and bananas. Its active form is pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP).

Class
Water-soluble vitamin (pyridoxine)
Evidence summary
Deficiency correction: established. Pregnancy nausea (with doxylamine): approved medicine. High intakes: nerve-toxicity concern.
Review status
Agent draft, 2026-10-31. Scientific review required.

What it does in the body

PLP is a coenzyme in more than 100 reactions, mostly in amino-acid metabolism. It is needed to make neurotransmitters such as serotonin, dopamine and GABA, and for hemoglobin synthesis.

Who may be at risk of deficiency

  • People with kidney disease or on dialysis.
  • Autoimmune conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis.
  • Alcohol use disorder.
  • Some medicines, such as isoniazid, reduce B6 status.
  • Deficiency can cause anemia, skin rash, cracks at the corners of the mouth and confusion.

Forms in supplements

Supplements usually contain pyridoxine hydrochloride; some contain pyridoxal 5'-phosphate.

What human trials show

  • Approved medicineA doxylamine–pyridoxine combination is approved in the United States and other countries for nausea and vomiting of pregnancy.
  • RandomizedB-vitamin trials lowering homocysteine (including B6) did not reduce cardiovascular events in people without deficiency.
  • RandomizedEvidence for premenstrual symptoms comes from small, low-quality trials.

Observational evidence

  • Low PLP is associated with inflammation and cardiovascular disease, but this may reflect disease rather than cause it.

Safety, upper limits and interactions

  • Long-term high intake from supplements can cause sensory neuropathy (numbness, loss of balance).
  • Australia's TGA has required neuropathy warnings on products above 10 mg/day since 2023, after reports of neuropathy below 50 mg/day.
  • In 2023 the European Food Safety Authority set a much lower adult upper level (12 mg/day) than the US value.

Reference values (NIH, adults)

ReferenceValue
Adults 19–50 (RDA)1.3 mg/day
Men 51+ / women 51+ (RDA)1.7 / 1.5 mg/day
US tolerable upper limit, adults100 mg/day
EFSA upper level, adults (2023)12 mg/day

Regulatory context

  • United States: dietary supplements do not need FDA approval for safety or effectiveness before sale; manufacturers are responsible for safety and label claims.
  • Brazil: food supplements are regulated by Anvisa (RDC 243/2018 and its normative instructions), which define permitted ingredients, minimum and maximum amounts and allowed claims.

Limits of the evidence

Upper limits differ between agencies because neuropathy data are inconsistent. Many multivitamins and energy drinks add B6, so total intake can be higher than expected.

This profile is educational. It does not recommend doses, products or brands. Talk to a health professional before starting supplements, especially if you take medicines, are pregnant or have a chronic condition.
