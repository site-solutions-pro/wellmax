Niacin (vitamin B3)

Niacin (vitamin B3) includes nicotinic acid and nicotinamide, found in meat, fish, peanuts and fortified grains. The body can also make niacin from the amino acid tryptophan.

Class
Water-soluble vitamin (B3: nicotinic acid, nicotinamide)
Evidence summary
Deficiency (pellagra): established. Niacin for heart disease on top of statins: no benefit, more harm. Nicotinamide for skin cancer in high-risk people: one positive trial.
Review status
Agent draft, 2026-10-31. Scientific review required.

What it does in the body

Niacin is converted to NAD and NADP, coenzymes in more than 400 reactions, including energy production, DNA repair and cell signaling. NAD levels fall with age in some tissues, which drives interest in NAD precursors.

Who may be at risk of deficiency

  • Pellagra (dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia) is rare where diets are varied but still occurs with alcohol use disorder and some malabsorption conditions.
  • Diets based mainly on untreated maize.
  • Carcinoid syndrome and long-term isoniazid use.

Forms in supplements

Nicotinic acid (immediate or extended release) lowers lipids at high amounts and causes flushing. Nicotinamide does not affect lipids or cause flushing. Nicotinamide riboside (NR) and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) are marketed as NAD precursors.

What human trials show

  • RandomizedAIM-HIGH and HPS2-THRIVE (together more than 28,000 patients on statins): adding niacin did not reduce cardiovascular events; HPS2-THRIVE found more serious side effects, including diabetes complications, infections and bleeding.
  • RandomizedONTRAC (386 people with prior skin cancers): nicotinamide reduced new non-melanoma skin cancers by 23% over 12 months.
  • Early humanNR and NMN raise blood NAD in small human trials, but clinical benefits have not been shown.

Observational evidence

  • NAD decline with aging is documented mainly in animal models and some human tissues.

Safety, upper limits and interactions

  • Nicotinic acid commonly causes flushing; high amounts can cause liver damage, high blood glucose and gout.
  • The upper limit is based on flushing and applies to supplements and fortified foods.
  • Nicotinic acid can add to the effects of blood-pressure medicines.

Reference values (NIH, adults)

ReferenceValue
Men (RDA)16 mg NE/day
Women (RDA)14 mg NE/day
Tolerable upper limit (supplements and fortified foods)35 mg/day

Regulatory context

  • United States: dietary supplements do not need FDA approval for safety or effectiveness before sale; manufacturers are responsible for safety and label claims.
  • Brazil: food supplements are regulated by Anvisa (RDC 243/2018 and its normative instructions), which define permitted ingredients, minimum and maximum amounts and allowed claims.

Limits of the evidence

Lipid improvement with niacin did not translate into fewer heart events. Anti-aging claims for NAD precursors are based mainly on animal studies.

This profile is educational. It does not recommend doses, products or brands. Talk to a health professional before starting supplements, especially if you take medicines, are pregnant or have a chronic condition.
