Performance

Training, recovery and adaptation

Strength and endurance improve when training load, recovery, sleep and nutrition are aligned. This hub separates what is proven in people from what is popular in gyms.

Key systems

Muscle adaptation

Progressive resistance training is the main signal for muscle and strength gains.

Aerobic capacity

Endurance training improves heart, blood vessels and mitochondrial capacity.

Recovery

Sleep, energy intake and load management shape how well tissues repair between sessions.

Tissue repair

Tendons and ligaments adapt slowly; most repair-peptide data come from animal models.

Evidence

What has the strongest human evidence

  • Progressive resistance training increases muscle mass and strength at any adult age.
  • Adequate total protein intake modestly enhances gains from resistance training in meta-analyses.
  • Sleep restriction impairs performance and recovery.
  • Repair peptides such as BPC-157 and TB-500 lack controlled human trials for sports injuries.

How to read the evidence

Many products marketed for recovery or muscle growth are supported mainly by animal or cell studies. Competitive athletes should also check anti-doping rules: several growth-hormone secretagogues are prohibited in sport.
