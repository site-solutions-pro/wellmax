Longevity

Living longer, and better

Healthspan is the number of years lived with good function. The best evidence comes from long human studies on fitness, cardiovascular risk and metabolic health; much of the newer molecular research is still early.

Key systems

Cardiovascular risk

Atherosclerosis is the leading cause of death worldwide and builds over decades.

Fitness and muscle

Aerobic capacity and strength are among the strongest predictors of survival in observational studies.

Mitochondria

Energy production and cellular stress responses change with age; this research is mostly preclinical.

Inflammation

Chronic low-grade inflammation is associated with many age-related diseases.

Evidence

What has the strongest human evidence

  • Higher cardiorespiratory fitness is associated with lower long-term mortality, with no clear upper limit in large cohorts.
  • Not smoking, blood-pressure control and lipid control reduce cardiovascular events in randomized trials.
  • Regular physical activity, including muscle strengthening, is recommended for all adults.
  • Adults should sleep at least seven hours per night on a regular basis.

How to read the evidence

A change in a biomarker is not the same as a longer life. Longevity claims need long follow-up and hard outcomes such as events and mortality; most anti-aging molecules have not reached that stage in humans.
