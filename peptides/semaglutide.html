Mechanism

GLP-1 receptor signaling

Semaglutide mimics endogenous GLP-1 activity with a prolonged pharmacokinetic profile. Clinical effects involve glucose-dependent insulin secretion, reduced glucagon signaling when glucose is elevated, slower gastric emptying and reduced energy intake.

GLP-1

Receptor agonism

Activates GLP-1 receptors in metabolic and appetite-regulating pathways.

PK

Long half-life

Structural modifications support prolonged exposure in approved weekly injectable formulations.

PD

Metabolic effects

Effects include improved glycemic control and reduced energy intake in studied populations.

Safety and limitations

Commonly reported events

In STEP 1, nausea and diarrhea were the most common adverse events and were generally transient and mild to moderate. Discontinuation because of gastrointestinal events occurred in 4.5% of participants receiving semaglutide and 0.8% receiving placebo.

Contraindications, boxed warnings, precautions, interactions and population-specific risks must be taken from current official prescribing information. Evidence from one formulation or indication should not be transferred automatically to another.
