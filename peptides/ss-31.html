SS-31 / Elamipretide

Elamipretide, historically known as SS-31 or MTP-131, is a mitochondria-targeted tetrapeptide that interacts with cardiolipin in the inner mitochondrial membrane. In the United States it has accelerated approval as Forzinity for improving muscle strength in adult and pediatric patients with Barth syndrome weighing at least 30 kg.

Evidence snapshot

Approved, indication-specific evidence

Regulatory authorization applies to a defined Barth syndrome population. Evidence from other mitochondrial diseases cannot be generalized to longevity, athletic performance or nonspecific mitochondrial optimization.

Overview

What SS-31 is — and what approval means

SS-31 is the research name commonly associated with elamipretide. FDA approval is limited to the labeled Barth syndrome indication and was granted through the accelerated approval pathway.

01

Molecule class

Cell-permeable aromatic-cationic tetrapeptide that localizes to the inner mitochondrial membrane and associates with cardiolipin.

02

Regulatory maturity

FDA-approved as Forzinity for a specific Barth syndrome population; confirmatory post-marketing research is required.

03

Broader research

Clinical programs have studied primary mitochondrial myopathy and other mitochondrial disorders, with mixed efficacy results.

04

Key boundary

An approval in Barth syndrome does not establish anti-aging, performance-enhancing or general mitochondrial benefits in healthy people.

Mechanism

Cardiolipin and mitochondrial membrane biology

Mechanistic plausibility is not equivalent to clinical benefit.

CL

Cardiolipin interaction

Elamipretide binds cardiolipin, a phospholipid enriched in the inner mitochondrial membrane and important to membrane organization and respiratory-chain function.

IMM

Membrane effects

Preclinical studies support effects on mitochondrial structure, energetics and oxidative stress. The magnitude and relevance of these effects depend on disease context.

Clinical

Human translation

Clinical benefit must be established separately for each disease, endpoint and population rather than inferred from mitochondrial biomarkers.

Human evidence

Barth syndrome and mitochondrial myopathy

Barth syndrome

Small randomized program

The pivotal Barth syndrome development program included a randomized crossover trial and long-term extension. The original randomized primary endpoints were not met, while longer-term data and additional evidence supported regulatory review.

FDA 2025

Accelerated approval

FDA granted accelerated approval based on improvement in knee extensor muscle strength, considered reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit.

PMM

Phase III negative primary endpoints

In MMPOWER-3, a randomized phase III trial in 218 participants with primary mitochondrial myopathy, elamipretide did not demonstrate statistically significant benefit on the co-primary endpoints.

Post-approval

Confirmation required

A randomized confirmatory Barth syndrome trial is required to verify clinical benefit under the accelerated approval program.

Safety

Known findings and remaining uncertainty

FDA identifies injection-site reactions among the most common adverse reactions observed in clinical development and notes that serious reactions have also been reported. Safety outside the approved population, long-term use across broader indications and rare harms require indication-specific evidence.

Regulatory status

Forzinity is approved in the United States

Verified Aug 8, 2026: FDA granted accelerated approval on September 19, 2025 for Forzinity (elamipretide) to improve muscle strength in adult and pediatric patients with Barth syndrome weighing at least 30 kg. FDA lists an ongoing post-marketing requirement for a randomized confirmatory trial. This status should not be extrapolated to other uses.
