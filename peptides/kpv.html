KPV

Lysine-proline-valine, the C-terminal tripeptide fragment of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH), investigated in inflammatory, epithelial, and antimicrobial laboratory models.

Overview

Identity and evidence boundary

KPV corresponds to residues 11–13 of α-MSH. Some anti-inflammatory actions have been reported without the pigmentary sequence, but a fragment should not be assumed to reproduce the parent hormone’s receptor pharmacology or clinical effects.

01

Molecule class

C-terminal tripeptide fragment of the melanocortin peptide α-MSH.

02

Research status

Preclinical exploratory research in cell, microbial, and animal inflammatory models. No established clinical-development program was identified.

03

Evidence level

Preclinical only for claimed health outcomes; no reliable participant efficacy or safety trials were identified.

04

Key boundary

Results from cells, isolated microbes, and induced colitis models are not evidence that KPV treats inflammatory or infectious disease in people.

Mechanism, receptors, and pathways

Supported signals and unresolved interpretation

Mechanistic plausibility is not clinical efficacy.

α-MSH

Parent relationship

KPV is the C-terminal α-MSH sequence. Sequence origin establishes relationship, not equivalent melanocortin-receptor activity.

PepT1

Epithelial transport

Cell and mouse colitis research implicated the oligopeptide transporter PepT1 in epithelial uptake and experimental effects.

NF-κB

Immune signaling

Laboratory work reported reduced pro-inflammatory signaling, including NF-κB-related readouts. This is a mechanistic observation, not a clinical endpoint.

Microbes

Antimicrobial models

Direct growth effects have been reported against selected organisms under laboratory conditions. In vitro antimicrobial activity is not treatment efficacy.

Pharmacology

PK and pharmacodynamics

PK

Pharmacokinetics

Reliable human pharmacokinetic data are unavailable. Experimental epithelial transport does not define systemic or tissue exposure, stability, metabolism, interactions, or human bioavailability.

PD

Pharmacodynamics

Changes in cytokine and inflammatory readouts in experimental systems are preclinical pharmacodynamic observations. No validated human target-engagement or clinical-response marker exists.

Human clinical evidence

No reliable participant trials identified

Reliable peer-reviewed human clinical trials establishing efficacy, safety, pharmacokinetics, or adverse-event rates were not identified. Human-derived cell systems are in vitro evidence, not human clinical evidence.

Preclinical animal evidence

Animal findings remain non-clinical

Mouse models of chemically induced intestinal inflammation reported improvements in experimental inflammatory and tissue endpoints. These models simplify disease biology and cannot establish benefit in ulcerative colitis, Crohn disease, or another human condition.

In vitro and ex vivo evidence

Epithelial-cell studies examined uptake and inflammatory signaling. Separate microbial assays reported activity against selected bacteria and fungi. Concentrations and controlled laboratory conditions do not establish achievable exposure or clinical antimicrobial benefit.

Mechanistic and theoretical boundary

Whether KPV acts mainly through PepT1-mediated uptake, intracellular signaling, melanocortin receptors, direct microbial effects, or context-dependent combinations remains unresolved. Claims of broad immune normalization or epithelial healing exceed the evidence.

Clinical development and principal studies

Research timeline

2008

Epithelial and colitis models

Current

Preclinical status

No reliable participant trial or confirmatory medicinal-development program was identified.

Needed

Translation

Human PK, target engagement, controlled efficacy, adequate safety, and long-term follow-up remain absent.

Safety findings and reported adverse events

Insufficient clinical safety characterization

Animal and cell studies cannot define human safety. Reliable adverse-event rates, maximum tolerated exposure, interactions, immunologic effects, reproductive toxicity, special-population risks, and long-term outcomes are unavailable. Antimicrobial activity also does not establish microbiome safety.

Evidence limitations

What the evidence cannot establish

Small or non-clinical evidence cannot determine clinical benefit, comparative effectiveness, suitable use, rare harms, or long-term outcomes. Product identity and quality are separate from molecule-level research.

Frequently asked questions

KPV FAQ

What is KPV’s relationship to α-MSH?

KPV is α-MSH’s three-amino-acid C-terminal fragment. This does not establish identical receptor activity or clinical effects.

Does KPV treat inflammatory bowel disease?

No reliable human trial evidence establishes that claim. Reported intestinal findings are from cells and animal models.

Is KPV an antibiotic?

No approved antimicrobial indication exists. Laboratory activity against selected organisms is not clinical efficacy.

Is it an approved medicine?

No FDA- or EMA-authorized medicinal indication was identified as of July 31, 2026.
