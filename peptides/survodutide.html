Survodutide

A long-acting peptide co-agonist of the glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and glucagon receptors under clinical investigation for obesity and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH).

Overview

What Survodutide is—and is not

Survodutide (development code BI 456906) is a synthetic dual-receptor agonist. It is a clinical research compound, not an approved medicine.

01

Molecule class

Long-acting glucagon/GLP-1 receptor dual agonist with a glucagon-derived peptide backbone.

02

Research status

Phase II findings have been peer reviewed; phase III programs are registered for obesity and MASH.

03

Evidence level

Moderate, indication-specific clinical evidence: randomized phase II trials, without an approval decision or mature long-term outcomes evidence.

04

Key boundary

Changes in weight, biomarkers, or liver histology do not by themselves establish long-term clinical benefit.

Mechanism and pathways

Dual receptor pharmacology

These receptor effects provide a biological rationale; they do not independently prove a health outcome.

GLP-1R

GLP-1 receptor agonism

Activates incretin signaling associated with glucose-dependent insulin secretion and reduced energy intake.

GCGR

Glucagon receptor agonism

Engages hepatic and systemic glucagon pathways implicated in energy expenditure and lipid metabolism, while glucagon signaling can also raise hepatic glucose output.

Bias

Designed balance

In vitro and animal characterization found greater potency at the human GLP-1 receptor than at the human glucagon receptor. The clinical importance of that balance remains mechanistic interpretation.

Integrated

Proposed net effect

The investigational premise is that appetite-related GLP-1 activity and glucagon-linked energy and liver effects may combine. Their individual contributions in humans are unresolved.

Pharmacology

What is established and unknown

PK

Pharmacokinetics

Early human studies and the phase II program support a prolonged exposure profile compatible with once-weekly study administration. A definitive public regulatory label does not exist, and reliable data for many special populations and interactions remain unavailable.

PD

Pharmacodynamics

Human trials observed reductions in body weight and changes in cardiometabolic and liver-related measures. These integrated responses cannot quantify how much each receptor contributed.

Model

In vitro evidence

Cell-based assays established agonism at human GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. Assay potency is not a clinical efficacy estimate.

Unknown

Unresolved questions

Long-term cardiovascular, hepatic, renal, gallbladder, pancreatic, and mortality outcomes—and rare adverse effects—are not established by the published phase II trials.

Human clinical evidence

Randomized phase II studies

Results apply to their enrolled populations, comparators, endpoints, and follow-up; they are not predictions for an individual.

Obesity

387 participants

A 46-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-finding trial enrolled adults with BMI ≥27 kg/m² without diabetes.

Primary outcome

Weight change

All prespecified Survodutide groups had greater mean percentage weight reduction than placebo at week 46; the largest reported adjusted mean change was −14.9%, versus −2.8% with placebo.

MASH

293 participants

A 48-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled adults with biopsy-defined MASH and fibrosis stages F1–F3.

Primary outcome

Histologic response

MASH improvement without worsening fibrosis occurred more often in each Survodutide group than with placebo. This is a surrogate histologic endpoint, not proof of fewer liver-related clinical events.

Evidence gap

No outcomes trial

Published evidence does not yet establish effects on cardiovascular events, liver decompensation, transplantation, cancer, or survival.

Preclinical evidence

Animal findings remain non-clinical

In diet-induced obese mice, BI 456906 reduced food intake and body weight and increased energy expenditure; investigators also studied hepatic lipid metabolism in rodent models. These controlled animal findings informed the development hypothesis but cannot establish human efficacy or safety.

Mechanistic versus theoretical claims

Mechanistic evidence: receptor activation is supported by cell assays, and metabolic responses were observed in animal models. Theoretical interpretation: claims that dual agonism will prevent cardiovascular or liver complications, outperform another molecule, or provide durable benefit after treatment ends remain unproven unless tested in suitable human trials.

Current

Confirmatory research

Official trial registries list phase III studies, including SYNCHRONIZE-1 (NCT06066515), SYNCHRONIZE-2 (NCT06066528), and the LIVERAGE MASH program. Completion and approval timelines cannot be inferred from registration.

Safety findings

Adverse events and limitations

In the obesity phase II trial, gastrointestinal disorders were the most frequent adverse events; nausea was the most common. Adverse-event-related discontinuation was more frequent across pooled Survodutide groups than with placebo. In the MASH trial, gastrointestinal adverse events were also more frequent with Survodutide than placebo.

These trials were not large or long enough to characterize uncommon or delayed harms. Group averages do not establish individual safety, and absence of a detected signal is not evidence that a risk is absent. There is no approved prescribing information defining contraindications, warnings, interactions, or use in special populations.

Regulatory status

Not an approved medicine

Verified July 31, 2026: Survodutide is investigational. No approval or authorized indication was identified in the official U.S. Food and Drug Administration or European Medicines Agency medicine resources. Trial registration is not marketing authorization.

This status may change after this review date. Consult current regulator databases and a qualified health professional for medical decisions. Products represented as Survodutide outside authorized clinical research do not acquire evidence of identity, quality, safety, or effectiveness from the published trials.

Frequently asked questions

Survodutide FAQ

Is Survodutide approved?

No. As verified July 31, 2026, it remains investigational and no FDA- or EMA-authorized indication was identified.

What is it being studied for?

Human programs include obesity and MASH. Evidence in one population or endpoint should not be generalized to another.

How does it differ from semaglutide?

Both activate GLP-1 receptors; Survodutide also activates glucagon receptors and remains investigational. This mechanistic distinction does not establish comparative superiority.

Do phase II results establish long-term benefit?

No. Weight and liver-histology findings require confirmation, and long-term patient-centered outcomes remain unresolved.

Is its safety fully known?

No. Gastrointestinal events were common in published trials, while rare, delayed, and population-specific risks require larger and longer studies.
