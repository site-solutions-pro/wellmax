Ipamorelin

Ipamorelin is a selective ghrelin-receptor agonist investigated for growth-hormone release and gastrointestinal motility. Human clinical development included a phase II randomized trial in postoperative ileus, but that study did not demonstrate statistically significant efficacy on its key endpoint.

Evidence snapshot

Human trial evidence, negative efficacy signal

A multicenter randomized phase II study enrolled 117 bowel-resection patients. Ipamorelin was generally well tolerated, but the primary efficacy comparison was not statistically significant.

Overview

A ghrelin mimetic with limited clinical development

01

Molecule class

Peptidomimetic agonist of the ghrelin/growth-hormone secretagogue receptor.

02

Human evidence

Clinical development included controlled evaluation for postoperative ileus after bowel resection.

03

Evidence level

Phase II human evidence exists, but clinical efficacy was not established in the published proof-of-concept study.

04

Key boundary

Growth-hormone release does not establish muscle gain, fat loss, recovery, sleep or anti-aging benefits.

Mechanism

Ghrelin-receptor agonism

GHSR

Receptor activation

Ipamorelin activates the growth-hormone secretagogue receptor, a pathway involved in GH release and gastrointestinal motility.

GH

Endocrine response

Preclinical and early pharmacology studies characterized GH-secretagogue activity, but endocrine response is not equivalent to a clinical outcome.

GI

Motility rationale

Ghrelin-receptor signaling can promote gastrointestinal motility, which motivated postoperative ileus trials.

Human evidence

Postoperative ileus phase II trial

117 enrolled

Randomized multicenter study

Adults undergoing bowel resection were randomized in a double-blind placebo-controlled phase II trial.

Primary endpoint

No significant difference

Median time to first tolerated standardized meal was numerically shorter with ipamorelin, but the difference versus placebo was not statistically significant.

Safety

Generally well tolerated

The published trial reported a similar overall burden of treatment-emergent adverse events between study groups.

Gap

No validated body-composition outcome

The clinical trial evidence does not establish bodybuilding, fat-loss, anti-aging or recovery benefits.

Safety

Limited development means limited certainty

Short-term phase II data cannot define uncommon or long-term risks, effects in healthy populations, or safety when used outside controlled research. Mechanistic selectivity should not be interpreted as proof of a superior safety profile.

Regulatory status

Investigational

Verified Aug 8, 2026: Ipamorelin is described here as an investigational research molecule. Clinical trial registration and published phase II research do not constitute an approved therapeutic indication.
