Tesamorelin

Tesamorelin is a growth hormone-releasing hormone analog that stimulates endogenous GH secretion and downstream IGF-1. FDA-licensed Egrifta products are indicated for reduction of excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV and lipodystrophy.

Evidence snapshot

Approved for a defined population

Phase III trials support reduction of visceral adipose tissue in adults with HIV-associated abdominal fat accumulation. This does not establish general weight-loss, bodybuilding, anti-aging or performance benefits.

Overview

Endogenous GH stimulation, not exogenous GH

01

Molecule class

Synthetic GHRH analog designed to activate pituitary GHRH receptors and increase pulsatile GH secretion.

02

Clinical evidence

Two multicenter randomized phase III programs enrolled more than 800 adults with HIV-associated abdominal adiposity.

03

Regulatory maturity

FDA Purple Book lists tesamorelin products Egrifta SV and Egrifta WR as prescription biologics under BLA 022505.

04

Key boundary

Reduction in visceral adipose tissue in HIV lipodystrophy should not be extrapolated to obesity treatment or longevity in otherwise healthy populations.

Mechanism

GHRH → GH → IGF-1 axis

Biological pathway activation is not itself evidence of a clinical benefit.

GHRHR

Pituitary signaling

Tesamorelin activates GHRH receptors on pituitary somatotrophs, increasing endogenous GH secretion.

IGF-1

Downstream response

Human trials demonstrate increases in circulating IGF-1 alongside changes in visceral adipose tissue.

Context

Disease-specific outcome

The approved evidence base concerns excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV-associated lipodystrophy, not generalized fat loss.

Human evidence

Randomized phase III trials

806 participants

Pooled phase III program

Two randomized placebo-controlled studies found a significant treatment effect on visceral adipose tissue at 26 weeks.

Durability

Effect depends on continued treatment

Extension data showed visceral fat reduction was sustained during continued therapy and reaccumulated after discontinuation.

Liver fat

Exploratory evidence

A smaller randomized study reported reductions in visceral and hepatic fat, but clinical-outcome significance remains uncertain.

Limit

No general anti-aging evidence

Trials do not establish improved lifespan, athletic performance or broad metabolic benefit in healthy people.

Safety

GH/IGF-1 pathway requires indication-specific monitoring

Published trials and product labeling identify adverse reactions and metabolic considerations that require clinical supervision. Elevation of IGF-1 and effects on glucose regulation are relevant safety considerations. The safety profile should be interpreted within the approved population and current prescribing information.

Regulatory status

FDA-licensed biologic

Verified Aug 8, 2026: FDA Purple Book lists tesamorelin under BLA 022505, with original approval on November 10, 2010. Egrifta SV and Egrifta WR are listed as prescription products. Approval is indication-specific and should not be generalized to other uses.
