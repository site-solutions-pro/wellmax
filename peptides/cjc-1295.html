Long-acting GHRH analog · investigational

CJC-1295

CJC-1295 is a synthetic growth hormone-releasing hormone analog engineered for prolonged exposure through binding to circulating albumin. Early controlled human studies demonstrated sustained increases in GH and IGF-1, but did not establish clinical efficacy for body composition, performance, anti-aging or disease outcomes.

Evidence snapshot

Physiologic signal, limited clinical evidence

Human studies document pharmacokinetics and endocrine responses in healthy adults. There is no established approved indication or mature outcomes evidence.

Overview

Designed for prolonged GHRH signaling

01

Molecule class

Long-acting synthetic GHRH analog designed to extend exposure relative to endogenous GHRH.

02

Human evidence

Small randomized placebo-controlled studies in healthy adults characterized endocrine response and pharmacokinetics.

03

Evidence level

Early human pharmacology evidence rather than disease-specific efficacy evidence.

04

Key boundary

Higher GH or IGF-1 concentrations do not by themselves demonstrate improved health, muscle gain, fat loss, recovery or longevity.

Mechanism

Extended activation of pituitary GHRH signaling

GHRHR

Pituitary stimulation

CJC-1295 acts through the GHRH receptor, increasing endogenous GH secretion.

Albumin

Prolonged exposure

The molecule was engineered to associate covalently with endogenous albumin, producing a markedly longer apparent half-life than native GHRH.

IGF-1

Downstream endocrine effect

Controlled studies in healthy adults demonstrated sustained increases in circulating IGF-1.

Human evidence

Early randomized endocrine studies

2006

Ascending-dose trials

Two randomized placebo-controlled studies in healthy adults showed prolonged GH and IGF-1 increases lasting several days after administration.

Pulsatility

GH rhythm preserved

A follow-up study found increased mean and trough GH secretion with preserved pulsatile secretion one week after exposure.

Small samples

No clinical-outcome proof

The studies were designed around endocrine pharmacology, not changes in strength, body composition, disease progression or lifespan.

Gap

Development maturity limited

No large confirmatory clinical program or approved therapeutic indication was identified in the primary evidence reviewed for this profile.

Safety

Short-term tolerance does not establish long-term safety

Early studies reported no serious adverse reactions in their limited samples, but these trials were too small and brief to characterize uncommon, long-term or population-specific risks. Sustained activation of the GH/IGF-1 axis requires cautious interpretation because endocrine biomarkers are not safety surrogates.

Regulatory status

Investigational research molecule

Verified Aug 8, 2026: CJC-1295 is presented here as an investigational molecule. Early human studies do not constitute regulatory approval, and claims related to bodybuilding, anti-aging or performance are not established by those trials.
