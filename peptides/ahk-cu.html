Overview

Identity and evidence boundary

AHK-Cu resembles GHK-Cu but substitutes alanine for glycine at the first position. Structural similarity and copper binding do not prove shared exposure, targets, safety, or efficacy.

01

Molecule class

Synthetic copper-binding tripeptide complex and GHK-Cu analog.

02

Research status

Early exploratory laboratory research. No reliable formal human clinical-development program was identified.

03

Evidence level

Very low certainty for human outcomes: direct evidence is principally ex vivo human follicles and cultured cells.

04

Key boundary

Direct AHK-Cu results must be separated from GHK-Cu and broader copper-peptide literature.

Mechanism, receptors, and pathways

Supported signals and unresolved interpretation

Mechanistic plausibility is not clinical efficacy.

Cu

Copper complex

AHK can coordinate copper, but reliable comparative binding, stability, and biological-distribution data are limited.

Cell

Dermal papilla cells

A direct study examined vascular endothelial growth factor expression in cultured human dermal papilla cells.

Follicle

Hair-follicle model

The same research used isolated human follicles maintained outside the body. Ex vivo elongation is not clinical hair growth.

Unknown

Receptors and pathways

No validated AHK-Cu-specific receptor or complete signaling pathway has been established. Proposed similarity to GHK-Cu remains analogy, not proof.

Pharmacology

PK and pharmacodynamics

PK

Pharmacokinetics

Reliable human or animal pharmacokinetic data—absorption, bioavailability, distribution, metabolism, elimination, interactions, and special populations—are unavailable.

PD

Pharmacodynamics

No validated human pharmacodynamic marker or exposure-response relationship is available. Laboratory VEGF expression is not a validated clinical surrogate for hair restoration.

Human clinical evidence

No established clinical outcome evidence

Direct experiments used human-derived follicles and cells outside the body; they were not clinical trials in participants. Reliable peer-reviewed trials establishing hair growth, skin repair, wound healing, or another clinical outcome were not identified.

Preclinical animal evidence

Animal findings remain non-clinical

Reliable direct animal efficacy and long-term toxicology evidence for AHK-Cu is sparse or unavailable. Evidence about GHK-Cu or unrelated copper complexes cannot fill this gap.

In vitro and ex vivo evidence

A primary study reported follicle elongation in isolated human hair follicles and increased VEGF expression in cultured dermal papilla cells. Model conditions, short duration, and laboratory endpoints prevent clinical inference.

Mechanistic and theoretical boundary

It is unresolved whether copper delivery, VEGF-related signaling, or another mechanism accounts for the laboratory observations. Equivalent efficacy to GHK-Cu, minoxidil, or any other molecule is unproven.

Safety findings and reported adverse events

Insufficient clinical safety characterization

No adequate clinical adverse-event dataset exists. Local irritation, sensitization, systemic exposure, copper-related effects, interactions, reproductive risk, and long-term effects have not been reliably characterized. It is incorrect to infer safety from tripeptide size, topical marketing, or GHK-Cu data.

Evidence limitations

What the evidence cannot establish

Small or non-clinical evidence cannot determine clinical benefit, comparative effectiveness, suitable use, rare harms, or long-term outcomes. Product identity and quality are separate from molecule-level research.

Evidence-based comparison and related molecules

Similar size does not mean equivalent biology

GHK-Cu

GHK-Cu substitutes glycine for AHK-Cu’s alanine and has a broader, though still limited, literature. Its findings are not interchangeable.

KPV

KPV derives from α-MSH and is investigated mainly in inflammatory and antimicrobial models, not as a copper-peptide equivalent.

Frequently asked questions

AHK-Cu FAQ

Is AHK-Cu the same as GHK-Cu?

No. They differ at the first amino acid. Analogy does not establish equivalent pharmacology or outcomes.

Has AHK-Cu grown hair in clinical trials?

Reliable participant trials establishing clinical hair growth were not identified. Direct evidence comes from laboratory models.

Are its pharmacokinetics known?

No reliable human pharmacokinetic profile was identified.

Is it an approved medicine?

No FDA- or EMA-authorized medicinal indication was identified as of July 31, 2026.
