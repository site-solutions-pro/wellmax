Overview

Identity and evidence boundary

GHK occurs naturally in human plasma, saliva, and urine and can coordinate copper(II). That biological identity does not establish that an administered GHK-Cu product produces a therapeutic outcome.

01

Molecule class

Endogenous copper-binding tripeptide complex.

02

Research status

Exploratory research spanning biochemical, cell, animal, and limited human topical studies; no established clinical-development program was identified.

03

Evidence level

Mechanistic and preclinical evidence predominate. Human evidence is limited, formulation-specific, and insufficient for therapeutic conclusions.

04

Key boundary

Endogenous function, laboratory activity, cosmetic appearance findings, and treatment efficacy are different propositions.

Mechanism, receptors, and pathways

Supported signals and unresolved interpretation

Mechanistic plausibility is not clinical efficacy.

Cu

Copper binding

GHK coordinates copper(II) and has been proposed to participate in copper transport or availability. The in-vivo importance of this role remains incompletely defined.

ECM

Matrix signaling

Fibroblast experiments report changes in collagen and extracellular-matrix processes. Cell-culture responses do not demonstrate wound closure or skin rejuvenation in people.

Repair

Tissue remodeling

Animal wound models support a remodeling hypothesis, but species, wound context, formulation, and exposure limit translation.

Inflammation

Inflammatory signaling

Laboratory studies report modulation of inflammatory and oxidative pathways. A receptor that accounts for a specific clinical anti-inflammatory effect has not been established.

Pharmacology

PK and pharmacodynamics

PK

Pharmacokinetics

Reliable human absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination, bioavailability, interaction, and special-population data for GHK-Cu are unavailable.

PD

Pharmacodynamics

Biochemical copper binding and cell responses are established experimental observations. A validated human pharmacodynamic marker linked to clinical benefit is unavailable.

Human clinical evidence

Limited, product-specific skin research

Small topical studies have reported changes in measures of aged or photodamaged skin. The accessible evidence is limited by older reports, small samples, formulation dependence, short follow-up, and sparse replication. Reliable controlled human evidence establishing wound-healing, hair-growth, systemic, or anti-inflammatory benefit was not identified.

Preclinical animal evidence

Animal findings remain non-clinical

Animal wound experiments and other injury models have reported changes in repair-related endpoints. These are non-clinical findings and cannot establish effectiveness, optimal exposure, or safety in humans.

In vitro and ex vivo evidence

Fibroblast and biochemical studies support copper binding and effects on collagen or matrix-related processes. These experiments do not reproduce an intact human organism or establish a cosmetic or medicinal outcome.

Mechanistic and theoretical boundary

It remains theoretical that broad laboratory effects translate into clinically meaningful regeneration, reduced inflammation, hair growth, or reversal of aging. Hair claims in particular should not be inferred from AHK-Cu or general copper-peptide research.

Clinical development and principal studies

Research timeline

1973

Isolation

GHK was isolated from human plasma during research on plasma factors affecting liver-cell growth.

1980

Copper hypothesis

A primary biochemical study proposed that the growth-modulating tripeptide may facilitate copper uptake into cells.

1988

Fibroblast research

Cultured-fibroblast work reported stimulation of collagen synthesis by GHK-Cu.

Current

Evidence gap

No confirmatory medicinal-development program or adequate long-term clinical evidence was identified.

Safety findings and reported adverse events

Insufficient clinical safety characterization

Published topical research does not provide a sufficiently large or long safety dataset to characterize uncommon, delayed, sensitization, systemic, pregnancy, pediatric, or interaction risks. Reliable compound-specific adverse-event rates and an approved prescribing label are unavailable. Absence of a reported signal is not evidence of safety.

Evidence limitations

What the evidence cannot establish

Small or non-clinical evidence cannot determine clinical benefit, comparative effectiveness, suitable use, rare harms, or long-term outcomes. Product identity and quality are separate from molecule-level research.

Regulatory status

No identified U.S. or EU medicinal approval

Verified July 31, 2026: GHK-Cu has no FDA- or EMA-authorized medicinal indication identified in official medicine databases. Cosmetic use or commercial availability is not medicinal approval, and “research use” is not authorization for human use.

Status can change. Consult current official regulator databases and qualified professionals for medical decisions.

Frequently asked questions

GHK-Cu FAQ

Is GHK-Cu an endogenous molecule?

GHK is endogenous and can bind copper. That does not prove that an administered preparation has a therapeutic benefit.

Does it heal wounds in people?

Reliable confirmatory human evidence establishing wound-healing efficacy is unavailable; animal and cell findings must remain preclinical.

Is hair growth established?

No robust direct human clinical evidence establishing hair-growth benefit was identified.

Is it an approved medicine?

No FDA- or EMA-authorized medicinal indication was identified as of July 31, 2026.
