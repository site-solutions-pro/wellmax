MOTS-c

MOTS-c is a 16-amino-acid peptide encoded within the mitochondrial 12S rRNA region. Research links endogenous MOTS-c signaling to metabolic stress responses, skeletal muscle biology and AMPK-associated pathways, but most intervention evidence remains preclinical.

Evidence snapshot

Preclinical foundation with emerging human research

Human studies have measured endogenous MOTS-c responses and a phase 2a interventional trial is registered, but published therapeutic efficacy in humans is not yet established.

Overview

A mitochondrial-encoded signaling peptide

MOTS-c expands the concept of mitochondrial signaling beyond energy production by acting as a peptide signal linked to cellular and systemic metabolic adaptation.

01

Origin

Encoded by a short open reading frame within mitochondrial 12S rRNA and identified as a biologically active 16-amino-acid peptide.

02

Research focus

Insulin sensitivity, skeletal muscle metabolism, metabolic stress, exercise adaptation and aging biology.

03

Evidence level

Strong mechanistic and animal evidence, limited direct human intervention evidence.

04

Key boundary

Animal improvements in metabolic fitness or physical capacity do not establish anti-aging or performance benefits in humans.

Mechanism

Metabolic stress signaling

AMPK

Energy sensing

Original discovery work linked MOTS-c to inhibition of folate-cycle-linked purine biosynthesis and activation of AMPK-associated metabolic responses.

Nucleus

Stress response

Experimental studies report stress-dependent nuclear translocation and changes in nuclear gene expression related to metabolism and proteostasis.

Muscle

Skeletal muscle

Muscle appears to be an important tissue in preclinical models, with reported effects on glucose handling and mitochondrial bioenergetics.

Evidence

What has been demonstrated

2015

Discovery and metabolism

The original Cell Metabolism study identified MOTS-c and reported improved metabolic homeostasis and insulin sensitivity in mouse models.

2021

Exercise biology

Experimental work reported that exercise increases endogenous MOTS-c expression in human skeletal muscle and circulation, alongside extensive mouse data on physical capacity.

Human physiology

Circulating peptide studies

Small human studies have measured changes in circulating MOTS-c around exercise. These observational or physiological findings do not demonstrate benefit from exogenous MOTS-c treatment.

2026

Phase 2a registered

ClinicalTrials.gov lists a randomized placebo-controlled phase 2a study evaluating investigational MOTS-c in adults with prediabetes and overweight/obesity. Results are not yet available.

Safety and limitations

Human safety is not established

Published preclinical studies cannot define human adverse-event frequency, long-term safety, interactions, contraindications or risks in specific populations. Registered clinical development should not be interpreted as evidence of efficacy or regulatory approval.

Regulatory status

Investigational

Verified Aug 8, 2026: MOTS-c is being studied in clinical research and is not presented here as an approved medicine. Trial registration indicates active development, not authorization or proven clinical benefit.
