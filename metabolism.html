Metabolism

How the body manages energy

Metabolism is a network: the liver, muscle, fat tissue, pancreas, gut and brain share signals that decide how energy is stored and used. This hub connects that biology to the markers and therapies being studied.

Key systems

Glucose regulation

Insulin and glucagon keep blood glucose in range by coordinating the liver, muscle and fat tissue.

Lipid transport

ApoB-containing particles carry cholesterol and triglycerides; their number drives atherosclerosis risk.

Appetite and incretins

Gut hormones such as GLP-1 and GIP signal to the pancreas and brain after meals.

Body composition

Visceral fat and muscle mass shape insulin sensitivity more than body weight alone.

Evidence

What has the strongest human evidence

  • Structured lifestyle programs with weight loss and regular activity reduced progression from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes in large randomized trials.
  • Regular physical activity improves insulin sensitivity independently of weight change.
  • Diet quality and overall energy balance matter more than any single nutrient or product.
  • Short or irregular sleep is associated with worse glucose regulation.

How to read the evidence

Approved incretin medicines have large randomized trials for specific indications. Most other metabolic claims rest on smaller or shorter studies, so each profile shows its evidence class separately.
