Evidence guide

Read scientific claims with the right level of confidence.

Evidence quality depends on study design, population, comparator, endpoint, duration, replication and relevance—not simply on whether a study exists.

1

In vitro

Cells, tissues or biochemical systems under controlled conditions.

2

Animal

Useful for hypotheses, toxicology and biological plausibility.

3

Early human

Initial safety, exposure, target engagement or exploratory outcomes.

4

Controlled trials

Comparative human evidence with defined endpoints.

5

Regulatory maturity

Authorization for a specific indication and jurisdiction.

Interpretation rules

What strong reading requires

P

Population

Who was studied, and how similar are they to the audience of interest?

C

Comparator

Placebo, active treatment, standard care or no comparator?

E

Endpoint

Clinical outcome, surrogate marker, imaging result or laboratory signal?

T

Time

Was follow-up long enough to assess durability and delayed risk?

Common mistakes

Avoid evidence inflation

Mechanism ≠ outcome

A plausible pathway does not prove a meaningful clinical benefit.

Association ≠ causation

Observed relationships may reflect confounding or reverse causality.

Surrogate ≠ clinical benefit

A marker can improve without proving better long-term outcomes.

Registration ≠ approval

A listed trial or commercial product does not establish authorization.
