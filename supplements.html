Supplements

Vitamins, minerals and fatty acids

Supplements can correct a deficiency, but extra intake rarely adds benefit when levels are already adequate. Each profile explains function, deficiency risk, study results and safety limits.

Principles

Correcting deficiency

The clearest benefit: B12 in deficiency, folate before pregnancy, vitamin D or iron when low.

Food first

Most nutrients are best obtained from a varied diet; supplements fill specific gaps.

Upper limits

Fat-soluble vitamins and some minerals can cause harm above tolerable upper intake levels.

Lab interactions

Some supplements change test results; biotin is a well-known example.

Evidence

Regulatory context

  • In the United States, supplements do not need FDA approval for safety or efficacy before they are sold.
  • In Brazil, food supplements are regulated by Anvisa under RDC 243/2018, which sets permitted ingredients, limits and claims.
  • A supplement label claim is not the same as evidence from randomized trials.
  • People who take medicines, are pregnant or have chronic conditions should review supplements with a health professional.
